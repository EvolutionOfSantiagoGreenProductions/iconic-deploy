Why Producers Leave Money on the Table

The music industry is built around royalty streams — but nobody explains all of them to independent producers. You register with ASCAP (maybe), you upload to DistroKid (maybe), and then you move on to making the next beat.

Meanwhile, mechanical royalties sit unclaimed at The MLC. Digital performance royalties pile up at SoundExchange. Your beats get used in YouTube videos that generate Content ID revenue you never see. International neighboring rights accumulate in territories you never knew existed.

This guide covers all 15. Each one has a real organization behind it, a real registration process, and real money waiting to be claimed. Most are free to register for. None of them come to you automatically.

Performance Royalties
01
ASCAP
Performance Royalties — Writer Share
PRO · Writer Registration

Every time your song is performed publicly — on streaming platforms, radio, live venues, TV, film — ASCAP collects a fee from whoever played it and distributes it to the songwriter. As a producer who co-writes the music, you're entitled to the writer's share.

This requires a separate ASCAP Writer account. If you only have a publisher account, you're missing half the money.

Action
Register as a Writer at ascap.com/music-creators · $50 one-time fee · Register each work after release
02
ASCAP
Performance Royalties — Publisher Share
PRO · Publisher Registration

ASCAP splits performance royalties 50/50 between the writer and the publisher. If you don't have a publishing entity registered, the publisher share goes uncollected. As an independent producer, you are your own publisher — but you have to register separately to collect that half.

The Publisher share is paid to a separate entity from the Writer share, which is why it requires a separate registration form and a publishing company name.

Action
Register a publishing entity separately at ASCAP · Name it "[Your Name] Music" or your production company · $50 one-time fee · Link works to both accounts
03
BMI
Performance Royalties (Alternative to ASCAP)
PRO · Free Registration

BMI is the other major U.S. Performing Rights Organization. You can only be a member of one PRO at a time (ASCAP or BMI, not both). BMI is free to join for songwriters and publishers, while ASCAP charges a one-time fee. Both collect the same types of royalties. Choose one and register all your works there.

Action
If not yet with ASCAP: register at bmi.com/creators · Free for songwriters · $150 for publisher registration
Mechanical Royalties
04
The MLC
Digital Mechanical Royalties
Mechanical · Free Registration

The Mechanical Licensing Collective was created by the Music Modernization Act (2018) to collect mechanical royalties from digital streaming services — Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, etc. These are paid per stream for the reproduction of your composition, separate from performance royalties.

The MLC has a database of unclaimed royalties. If your music is streaming and you're not registered, that money is held in their unmatched pool. Registration is free and retroactive claims are possible.

Action
Register your works at themlc.com · Free · Required separately from your PRO · Register each released work with ISRC and UPC
05
DistroKid / Distribution Platform
Publishing / Mechanical Admin
Distribution · Publishing Admin

DistroKid (and similar distributors) can register your compositions with publishing admin services that collect mechanical royalties internationally — including markets The MLC doesn't cover. DistroKid's "Publishing" add-on registers with sub-publishers in 100+ countries. Without this, foreign mechanical royalties are uncollected.

Key metadata that must be correct: ISRC codes, song title spelling, composer and publisher names, and split percentages.

Action
In DistroKid: enable Publishing Admin for each release · Verify ISRC codes are generated and recorded · Confirm songwriter credits and publisher listed accurately
06
Harry Fox Agency / Songfile
Physical & Download Mechanical Royalties
Mechanical · Physical Media

If your music appears on physical media (CDs, vinyl, cassettes) or is sold as a download, mechanical royalties are owed to the songwriter/publisher per unit. HFA licenses these on behalf of publishers and collects royalties from labels and physical distributors. Less relevant for streaming-only releases, but important if you have any physical distribution deal.

Action
If you have physical releases or download sales: register at harryfox.com as a publisher · Submit your catalog
Digital Performance Royalties
07
SoundExchange
Digital Performance Royalties (Master Owner)
Digital Performance · Free Registration

SoundExchange collects royalties specifically for master recording owners and featured artists when music plays on non-interactive digital platforms — satellite radio (SiriusXM), internet radio (Pandora, iHeartRadio), and certain streaming modes. These are paid to the master owner and the featured artist separately.

This is completely separate from your PRO. Your ASCAP registration does NOT collect SoundExchange royalties. Millions of dollars in SoundExchange royalties go unclaimed every year because producers don't know this exists.

Action
Register free at soundexchange.com as both the Rights Owner and Featured Artist · Submit your catalog · Check for unclaimed royalties immediately
Copyright Protection & Legal
08
U.S. Copyright Office
Federal Copyright Registration
Legal Protection · $45–$65 per filing

Copyright exists automatically when you create a work, but federal registration is required to sue for statutory damages and attorney's fees in court. Without registration before infringement occurs, you can only recover actual damages — which are often impossible to prove. Registering within 3 months of publication or before infringement preserves the full range of legal remedies.

For producers: register both the musical composition (melody/harmony) and the sound recording (the actual beat file) — these are two separate copyrights.

Action
Register at copyright.gov/registration · eCO online system · $45 for single work, $65 for group of unpublished works · File within 3 months of release
Sync Licensing & Placement
09
Direct Licensing / Music Libraries
Sync Licensing — Film, TV, Ads, Games
Sync · Placement Fee + Backend Royalties

Sync licensing pays an upfront placement fee when your music is used in visual media — TV shows, films, commercials, YouTube channels, games, trailers — plus backend royalties through your PRO every time that content airs. Sync deals can range from $100 for an indie YouTube video to six figures for a national TV ad.

To pursue sync: your music must be cleared (no uncleared samples), properly registered with your PRO and copyright office, and available in stems (separated audio tracks). Libraries like Musicbed, Artlist, and Epidemic Sound license beats for a revenue share model.

Action
Ensure all beats are sample-free or fully cleared · Export stems · Submit to sync libraries: Musicbed, Artlist, Pond5, Musicbed, or directly pitch to music supervisors · Register each placed work with ASCAP immediately
YouTube & Digital Content
10
YouTube / DistroKid / AdRev
YouTube Content ID Monetization
Content ID · Ad Revenue Share

YouTube Content ID scans every video uploaded to YouTube and matches audio fingerprints against registered tracks. When a match is found, the rightsholder can claim ad revenue from that video. If you're a beat producer and someone uploads a song using your beat, Content ID can automatically capture that video's ad revenue on your behalf.

Access requires registering through a Content ID partner — DistroKid's "YouTube Money" add-on, AdRev, Identifyy, or a distributor that includes it. Direct access to Content ID is not available to most independent artists without a partner.

Action
Enable YouTube Money in DistroKid, or register with AdRev / Identifyy · Submit your catalog fingerprints · Note: this will claim videos of people who used your beats — factor into your beat license terms
11
YouTube
Channel Ad Revenue (Direct)
Ad Revenue · YouTube Partner Program

Your own YouTube channel generates ad revenue directly through the YouTube Partner Program once you hit 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours. As a beat producer, every beat video, visualizer, and compilation is a monetizable asset. This is direct payment for views on your own uploads, separate from Content ID claims.

Action
Apply for YPP at youtube.com/monetization once threshold is met · Enable monetization on all existing videos · Set up AdSense account linked to your channel
International Royalties
12
International PROs via Reciprocal Agreements
International Performance Royalties
Performance · International Collection

ASCAP and BMI have reciprocal agreements with performance rights organizations in 100+ countries. When your music plays in Germany, Japan, the UK, or Brazil, those countries' PROs collect royalties and forward them to ASCAP/BMI, which pays them out to you. This is automatic through your PRO membership — but only for works that are registered.

Unregistered works generate international royalties that may go to the "black box" — distributed to other rights holders or kept by the collecting society. Register every work.

Action
Register all works with ASCAP or BMI with complete metadata (ISWC numbers are assigned automatically) · Verify your international address and banking info is current in your PRO account
13
Neighbouring Rights Organizations
Neighboring Rights — Master Recording Performance
Neighboring Rights · International

Most countries outside the U.S. pay "neighboring rights" — performance royalties for the master recording (not just the composition) when it plays on radio and TV. The U.S. doesn't have a terrestrial radio performance right for masters, but if your music plays on international radio or TV, these royalties are owed to you as the master rights holder.

These are collected by organizations like PPL (UK), GVL (Germany), SENA (Netherlands), and others. Many independent producers never register with these organizations, and the royalties sit uncollected or are redistributed.

Action
Register with PPL (UK) if your music plays in the UK · For other territories, use a neighboring rights agency like Artisten Info or a distributor that includes neighboring rights collection
Direct Beat Sales & Licensing
14
Beatstars / Airbit / Direct
Beat License Sales (Exclusive & Non-Exclusive)
Direct Sales · License Revenue

Selling beats directly — non-exclusive leases, trackouts, exclusive rights — is a direct revenue stream independent of royalties. Non-exclusive licenses ($30–$200) let multiple artists use the beat simultaneously. Exclusive licenses ($300–$5,000+) transfer the right to one buyer, which typically ends non-exclusive licensing. Platforms like Beatstars and Airbit handle the storefront, contracts, and delivery automatically.

Every beat license should include proper songwriter credits in the contract so buyers can register correctly with their PROs — this protects both parties.

Action
Set up a storefront on Beatstars or Airbit · Create tiered license options (MP3 lease → trackout → exclusive) · Include songwriter credit requirements in every contract template
15
Streaming Platforms (via Distributor)
Master Recording Streaming Royalties
Streaming · Master Royalties

When you upload an instrumental beat to Spotify, Apple Music, Tidal, or any streaming platform through a distributor like DistroKid, you earn master royalties — currently approximately $0.003–$0.005 per stream on Spotify. These are paid to whoever owns the master recording (you, as the producer).

This is separate from mechanical royalties (The MLC) and performance royalties (ASCAP). The same stream generates all three. Most producers upload their beats and collect only this one, missing the other two. All three should be set up and registered for every release.

Action
Distribute every beat to all platforms via DistroKid · Verify banking/payment info · Monitor monthly statements · Cross-register each release with The MLC and ASCAP for full royalty coverage
